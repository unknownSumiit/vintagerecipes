How to Preserve Your Family’s Handwritten Recipes

By the Vintage Recipe Box team · Updated November 16, 2026 · 5 min read

A handwritten recipe card is more than instructions for a cake or a casserole. The handwriting, the splatters, and the notes in the margins are a connection to the people who cooked before us. Unfortunately, paper fades, ink smudges, and recipe boxes get lost. Here are practical ways to keep your family’s recipes safe and share them with the next generation.

1. Make digital copies first

Before anything else, make a copy of every card. The easiest method is to photograph each one with your smartphone:

  • Lay the card flat on a plain, light-colored surface near a window in daylight, but out of direct sun.
  • Hold the phone directly above the card so it is not tilted.
  • Photograph the back too, since many cooks wrote notes there.
  • On an iPhone, the Notes app can scan documents: open a note, tap the camera icon, and choose Scan Documents. It automatically straightens and crops the card.

Save the photos in at least two places, such as your phone’s photo library and a cloud service or a USB drive, so a single lost device does not mean lost recipes.

2. Protect the original cards

  • Store cards in acid-free photo sleeves or sheet protectors, which are available at office supply and craft stores.
  • Keep them away from heat, moisture, and sunlight. A kitchen drawer near the stove is not the best home for an heirloom card.
  • Avoid tape, rubber bands, and paper clips, which can stain and damage paper over time.
  • When cooking, use a printed copy instead of the original.

3. Write down the stories

The memories behind a recipe are as valuable as the recipe itself. Next to each one, jot down who made it, when it was served, and any family stories that go with it. Was it always at Christmas? Did Grandpa insist on extra raisins? If older relatives are still with you, ask them about the recipes now. Recording their answers on your phone is a wonderful keepsake in itself.

4. Create a family cookbook

A family cookbook makes a treasured gift for weddings, graduations, and holidays. You can:

  • Print the scanned cards and assemble them in a simple binder with sheet protectors.
  • Use an online photo book service to create a bound book with pictures of the original cards, typed versions, and family photos.
  • Invite relatives to contribute their own favorite recipes and memories.

5. Keep cooking them

The best way to preserve a recipe is to keep making it. Cook family recipes with children and grandchildren, and let them help measure and stir. A recipe that is cooked and shared stays alive far longer than one that sits in a box.
