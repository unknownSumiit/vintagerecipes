Lemon Meringue Pie

By the Vintage Recipe Box team · Updated November 16, 2026

Classic lemon meringue pie with a bright, tangy lemon filling and a tall, golden meringue topping that does not weep or shrink.

1800s classic
📜 A little history · 1800s classic

The American lemon meringue pie is often credited to Elizabeth Goodfellow, who ran a cooking school in Philadelphia in the early 1800s. By the 20th century it was a diner counter and church supper favorite, admired for its sunny, tart filling and its tall, toasted crown of meringue.

Lemon meringue pie is the queen of the old-fashioned dessert table: a tart, glossy lemon filling topped with a mountain of sweet, toasted meringue.

It has a reputation for being tricky, but two simple steps make all the difference: spread the meringue over the filling while it is still hot, and seal the meringue all the way to the crust. Do that, and your meringue will not shrink or weep.

Instructions

  1. Heat the oven to 350°F.
  2. In a medium saucepan, whisk the sugar, cornstarch, and salt, then whisk in the water. Cook over medium heat, stirring constantly, until the mixture is thick and bubbling, 5 to 8 minutes.
  3. In a bowl, whisk the egg yolks. Slowly whisk in about 1 cup of the hot mixture to warm the yolks, then pour the yolk mixture back into the saucepan.
  4. Cook, stirring, for 2 minutes more while it bubbles gently. Remove from the heat and stir in the butter, lemon juice, and zest. Pour the hot filling into the baked crust.
  5. For the meringue, beat the egg whites and cream of tartar with a mixer until soft peaks form. Beat in the sugar 1 tablespoon at a time, then the vanilla, until the meringue is stiff and glossy.
  6. Spread the meringue over the hot filling, making sure it touches the crust all the way around to seal it. Make swirls and peaks with the back of a spoon.
  7. Bake for 12 to 15 minutes, until the meringue is golden.
  8. Cool on a rack for 1 hour, then refrigerate for at least 3 hours before slicing.

Tips for success

  • Egg whites whip best in a perfectly clean, grease-free bowl with no trace of yolk.
  • Dip your knife in hot water and wipe it dry between slices for clean cuts through the meringue.

Storing and reheating

Refrigerate, loosely covered, and enjoy within 2 days. Meringue pies do not freeze well.
