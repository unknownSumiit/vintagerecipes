Classic Double-Crust Apple Pie

By the Vintage Recipe Box team · Updated November 16, 2026

Classic homemade apple pie with tender cinnamon-spiced apples in a flaky, golden double crust.

Timeless
📜 A little history · Timeless

Apple pie came to America with English and Dutch settlers, and apple orchards soon spread across the country. By the early 1900s the phrase “as American as apple pie” was in common use, and during World War II, soldiers were said to tell reporters they were fighting “for mom and apple pie.”

Nothing says home quite like a warm slice of apple pie. This classic version is piled high with tender, cinnamon-spiced apples in a flaky golden crust, exactly the way it was made in farmhouse kitchens.

A mix of apples gives the best result: tart Granny Smith for tang and firm texture, plus a sweeter apple such as Honeycrisp, Golden Delicious, or Jonagold.

Instructions

  1. Place a rack in the lower third of the oven and heat to 425°F.
  2. In a large bowl, toss the apples with the sugars, flour, cinnamon, nutmeg, salt, and lemon juice.
  3. Fit one crust into a 9-inch pie plate. Pile in the apples, mounding them in the center, and dot with butter.
  4. Top with the second crust. Trim, fold the edges under, and crimp. Cut several slits in the top and brush with the egg wash.
  5. Set the pie on a foil-lined baking sheet. Bake for 20 minutes, then reduce the oven to 375°F and bake 35 to 45 minutes more, until the crust is golden and the juices bubble through the slits. Cover the edges with foil if they brown too fast.
  6. Cool for at least 3 hours so the juices thicken before slicing.

Tips for success

  • Slice the apples evenly so they cook at the same rate.
  • Serve warm with vanilla ice cream, or with a slice of sharp cheddar cheese, an old New England tradition.

Storing and reheating

Keep covered at room temperature for up to 2 days or refrigerate for up to 5 days. Rewarm slices in a 325°F oven for 10 minutes.
