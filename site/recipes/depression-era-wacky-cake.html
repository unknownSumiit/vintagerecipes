Depression-Era Wacky Cake

By the Vintage Recipe Box team · Updated November 16, 2026

Moist chocolate wacky cake made without eggs, butter, or milk and mixed right in the pan. The famous Depression-era recipe.

1930s–40s
📜 A little history · 1930s–40s

Wacky cake, also called crazy cake or Depression cake, is a moist chocolate cake made without eggs, butter, or milk. It became popular during the hard years of the 1930s and the rationing of World War II, when those ingredients were scarce or expensive. Vinegar and baking soda make it rise, and it is famously mixed right in the baking pan.

Prep10 min
Cook35 min
Total45 min
Serves9
Jump to Recipe

This remarkable little cake proves that hard times can produce wonderful things. It contains no eggs, no butter, and no milk, yet it is moist, chocolatey, and tender.

The fun part is how it is made: you stir the dry ingredients in the baking pan, make three wells, and pour in the oil, vinegar, and vanilla. No mixing bowl needed. It is also a handy recipe for anyone avoiding eggs or dairy.

Ingredients

Instructions

  1. Heat the oven to 350°F.
  2. In an ungreased 8-inch square baking pan, stir together the flour, sugar, cocoa, baking soda, and salt with a fork.
  3. Make three wells in the dry ingredients: one large, one medium, and one small.
  4. Pour the oil into the large well, the vinegar into the medium well, and the vanilla into the small well.
  5. Pour the cold water over everything and stir with the fork until smooth, making sure to reach the corners and bottom of the pan.
  6. Bake for 30 to 35 minutes, until a toothpick inserted in the center comes out clean.
  7. Cool in the pan. Dust with powdered sugar or spread with your favorite chocolate frosting.

Tips for success

  • The vinegar reacts with the baking soda to make the cake rise. You will not taste it once the cake is baked.
  • For a 9x13-inch pan, use the 2× button and bake for 30 to 35 minutes.

Storing and reheating

Cover and keep at room temperature for up to 3 days. The cake stays moist and freezes well, wrapped tightly, for up to 3 months.
