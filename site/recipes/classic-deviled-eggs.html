Classic Deviled Eggs

By the Vintage Recipe Box team · Updated November 16, 2026

Creamy, tangy classic deviled eggs with mayonnaise, mustard, and a sprinkle of paprika, plus the foolproof way to boil and peel eggs.

Potluck classic
📜 A little history · Potluck classic

In the 1700s, “deviled” came to describe foods made zesty with spices or mustard. Stuffed eggs had been around for centuries, but deviled eggs became a true American institution in the mid-1900s, when no potluck, picnic, or holiday buffet was complete without a special egg plate lined with them.

Prep20 min
Cook15 min
Total35 min
Makes24 deviled eggs
Jump to Recipe

There is a reason deviled eggs are always the first thing to disappear from the potluck table. This classic version is creamy, tangy, and just a little sweet from a spoonful of pickle relish.

The real secret is perfectly cooked eggs. Our hands-off method gives you tender whites and bright yellow yolks without the gray-green ring.

Classic Deviled Eggs

Batch size
24 deviled eggs

Ingredients

Instructions

  1. Place the eggs in a single layer in a large pot and cover with cold water by 1 inch. Bring to a full boil over high heat.
  2. As soon as the water boils, cover the pot, remove it from the heat, and let the eggs stand for 12 minutes.
  3. Transfer the eggs to a bowl of ice water for 10 minutes. Gently crack and peel them under cool running water.
  4. Slice the eggs in half lengthwise. Pop the yolks into a bowl and arrange the whites on a platter.
  5. Mash the yolks with a fork until fine. Stir in the mayonnaise, mustard, vinegar, relish, salt, and pepper until smooth.
  6. Spoon or pipe the filling into the whites. Sprinkle with paprika. Refrigerate until serving.

Tips for success

  • Eggs that are a week or two old peel much more easily than very fresh eggs.
  • For a neat finish, spoon the filling into a zip-top bag, snip off a corner, and pipe it into the whites.
  • Keep deviled eggs refrigerated, or set the platter over a tray of ice at a buffet. Do not leave them out for more than 2 hours.

Storing and reheating

Refrigerate, covered, for up to 2 days. You can boil and peel the eggs and make the filling a day ahead, storing them separately, then fill just before serving.
