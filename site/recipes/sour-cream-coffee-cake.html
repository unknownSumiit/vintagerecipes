Sour Cream Coffee Cake

By the Vintage Recipe Box team · Updated November 16, 2026

Moist, tender sour cream coffee cake baked in a Bundt pan with a cinnamon, brown sugar, and pecan swirl and a simple glaze.

1950s–60s
📜 A little history · 1950s–60s

The fluted Bundt pan was introduced in Minnesota in 1950, and by the 1960s a tall, tender coffee cake swirled with cinnamon and nuts was the pride of church coffee hours, bridge clubs, and neighborly visits. Sour cream was the secret to keeping these cakes moist for days.

Prep20 min
Cook55 min
Total1 hr 30 min
Serves12
Jump to Recipe

This is the coffee cake that was always on the table when friends stopped by: tall, tender, and rich from sour cream, with a ribbon of cinnamon, brown sugar, and pecans running through the middle.

It keeps beautifully for several days, which makes it a perfect cake to bake for guests, a church gathering, or a neighbor.

Sour Cream Coffee Cake

  • For the cake

Instructions

  1. Heat the oven to 350°F. Generously grease and flour a 10- or 12-cup Bundt pan.
  2. In a small bowl, mix the brown sugar, nuts, and cinnamon for the swirl.
  3. In a medium bowl, whisk together the flour, baking powder, baking soda, and salt.
  4. In a large bowl, beat the butter and sugar with a mixer until light and fluffy, about 3 minutes. Beat in the eggs one at a time, then the vanilla.
  5. On low speed, add the flour mixture in three parts, alternating with the sour cream, mixing just until combined.
  6. Spread half of the batter in the pan. Sprinkle evenly with the cinnamon mixture, then spread the remaining batter on top. Run a butter knife through the batter in a zigzag to swirl.
  7. Bake for 50 to 60 minutes, until a toothpick inserted in the cake comes out clean.
  8. Cool in the pan for 15 minutes, then turn out onto a rack. Whisk the powdered sugar and milk into a glaze and drizzle over the cooled cake.

Tips for success

  • Grease every ridge of the Bundt pan with soft butter or baking spray with flour so the cake releases cleanly.
  • Have the butter, eggs, and sour cream at room temperature so the batter mixes smoothly.

Storing and reheating

Keep covered at room temperature for up to 4 days. Freeze the unglazed cake, wrapped well, for up to 3 months.
