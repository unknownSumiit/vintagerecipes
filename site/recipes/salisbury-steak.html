Salisbury Steak with Mushroom Gravy

By the Vintage Recipe Box team · Updated November 16, 2026

Homemade Salisbury steak: tender seasoned beef patties simmered in a rich onion and mushroom gravy. Much better than the TV-dinner version.

1950s
📜 A little history · 1950s

Salisbury steak is named for Dr. James Salisbury, a 19th-century American physician who recommended ground beef patties to his patients. The dish became a household name in the 1950s and 1960s as a diner and TV-dinner favorite, but it has always been at its best homemade, smothered in onion and mushroom gravy.

Prep15 min
Cook30 min
Total45 min
Serves4
Jump to Recipe

If your only memory of Salisbury steak is a foil TV-dinner tray, this homemade version will be a pleasant surprise. Juicy, well-seasoned beef patties are browned, then simmered in a deep, savory gravy full of mushrooms and onions.

It is a complete comfort-food dinner in about 45 minutes. Serve it with mashed potatoes and green beans to soak up every drop of gravy.

  • For the patties
  • For the mushroom gravy

Instructions

  1. In a bowl, mix the beef, breadcrumbs, egg, chopped onion, ketchup, Worcestershire sauce, salt, and pepper until just combined. Shape into 4 oval patties about 1/2 inch thick.
  2. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Brown the patties for 3 to 4 minutes per side. Transfer to a plate.
  3. Reduce the heat to medium. Add the butter, mushrooms, and sliced onion to the skillet. Cook, stirring, until softened and golden, 6 to 8 minutes.
  4. Sprinkle the flour over the vegetables and stir for 1 minute. Gradually stir in the broth and Worcestershire sauce, scraping the bottom of the pan. Simmer until the gravy thickens, about 3 minutes.
  5. Return the patties to the skillet and spoon the gravy over them. Cover and simmer for 12 to 15 minutes, until the patties reach 160°F inside. Season the gravy with salt and pepper before serving.

Tips for success

  • Press a shallow dimple into the center of each patty before cooking so they stay flat instead of puffing up.
  • A teaspoon of browning sauce, such as Kitchen Bouquet, gives the gravy a deep old-fashioned color if you like.

Storing and reheating

Refrigerate in the gravy for up to 4 days. Freeze for up to 3 months. Reheat covered on the stove or in the microwave until hot.
