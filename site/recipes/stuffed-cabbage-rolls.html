Grandma’s Stuffed Cabbage Rolls

By the Vintage Recipe Box team · Updated November 16, 2026

Tender cabbage leaves wrapped around a seasoned beef, pork, and rice filling, baked in a sweet and tangy tomato sauce.

Immigrant classic
📜 A little history · Immigrant classic

Stuffed cabbage came to America with immigrants from Poland, Ukraine, Hungary, and across Eastern Europe, where it goes by names like gołąbki and holubtsi. In the steel and mill towns of Pennsylvania, Ohio, and the Midwest, these rolls became a beloved Sunday dinner and a fixture at church fundraisers and weddings.

Stuffed cabbage rolls take a little time, but they are a true labor of love and one of the most comforting dishes in any family recipe box. The cabbage leaves turn silky and tender as they bake, and the sweet-and-sour tomato sauce soaks into the savory filling.

They are even better the next day, and they freeze beautifully, so many cooks make a double batch.

Grandma’s Stuffed Cabbage Rolls

Batch size
6 servings (about 12 rolls)

When you change the batch size, pan sizes and cooking times may need adjusting.

  • For the filling
  • For the sauce

Instructions

  1. Bring a large pot of water to a boil. Cut out the core of the cabbage with a small knife. Lower the whole head into the water. As the outer leaves soften, after 2 to 3 minutes, gently peel them off with tongs. Continue until you have 12 large leaves. Drain and let cool.
  2. With a paring knife, shave down the thick rib on the back of each leaf so it rolls easily.
  3. Heat the oven to 350°F. In a bowl, stir together the crushed tomatoes, tomato sauce, brown sugar, and lemon juice. Spread about 1 cup of the sauce in the bottom of a 9x13-inch baking dish.
  4. In a large bowl, mix all of the filling ingredients with your hands until combined.
  5. Place about 1/3 cup of filling near the stem end of each leaf. Fold in the sides and roll up snugly. Place the rolls seam side down in the baking dish.
  6. Pour the remaining sauce over the rolls. Cover tightly with foil.
  7. Bake for 1 hour and 15 minutes, until the cabbage is very tender and the filling reaches 160°F inside. Let rest 10 minutes before serving.

Tips for success

  • Chop any leftover cabbage and tuck it under the rolls in the baking dish. It cooks down into the sauce beautifully.
  • Freezing the whole head of cabbage overnight, then thawing it, softens the leaves so they peel off without boiling.

Storing and reheating

Refrigerate for up to 4 days. Freeze in their sauce for up to 3 months. Reheat covered at 350°F for 30 to 40 minutes, or individually in the microwave.
